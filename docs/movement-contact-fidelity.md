# Contact recovery and continuous steering

This change fixes observed prototype failures using a custom local policy. It does not reproduce Blizzard's collision priority, surround planning, or avoidance solver. Native claims remain bounded to the historical captures documented in [arrival](native-arrival-fidelity.md), [Tank turns](tank-turn-fidelity.md), and [compact formations](compact-formation-fidelity.md).

An explicit twelve-SCV attack could leave workers behind a stationary firing front. Their intended velocity remained large while collision settling cancelled their displacement. The old stuck counter inspected displacement before collision, so it never noticed that jam. Symmetric contact settling could also push an attacker back into a held target after that target's pair had already been resolved. Finally, Tank avoidance changed its small steering angle every update; stationary alignment restarted for those corrections and repeatedly interrupted translation.

Melee recovery now monitors progress toward its current approach point across updates, after the previous collision pass. After 0.6 seconds without at least 0.5 world units of further progress, it samples up to 64 contact faces. Faces must be open in terrain and leave ordinary physical clearance from nearby bodies. Future faces are only treated as occupied when their claimant is already near the contact circle; a distant, blocked claimant cannot monopolize the far side of the target. Recovery begins after a demonstrated stall rather than eagerly assigning every approaching worker a slot.

A blocked approach takes a short annular leg around the target and nearby stationary firing allies. If no face fits, the worker waits and checks again at most every 0.2 seconds. This stops the rear line from continuously pushing the firing front. A dead front attacker releases space, and a target that leaves the local neighborhood restores ordinary pursuit immediately. Recovery preserves the actual enemy target and original order; direct Move abandons it immediately. Ranged pursuit, building-footprint interactions, harvesting collision bypass, solo point braking, and formation slot assignment continue through their existing paths.

Stationary fighting allies receive contact priority against a moving ally, while ordinary idle allies can still yield. Explicit Hold, Siege and transformation anchors receive a final physical projection after ordinary pair settling, preventing later pairs from undoing those contacts. This projection respects terrain and world bounds; it cannot solve contradictory overlaps trapped between multiple immutable bodies.

Tank stationary alignment now begins for a new order or target. After that intent starts translating, continuous avoidance uses the moving turn rate instead of starting another stationary alignment for every small correction. The captured east/north/west starts and moving reversal timings still pass. The precise native rule for stationary turns during an existing moving pursuit or a changing navigation corridor remains unmeasured; this change is a responsiveness correction, not a claim of native equality for those cases.

The recovery durations, sampling density, progress threshold, annular margin and friendly contact priority are custom tuning choices. They are not copied from SC2 catalog data. Source-backed unit radii, range, acceleration, stationary/moving turn rates and 22.4-loop Faster schedule remain unchanged.

`node tests/movement-contact-fidelity.mjs` uses the real combat, movement and navigation modules at 22.4 loops per second. It checks two twelve-SCV approaches with at least six contributing attackers and legal contacts, stable full-surround waiting, vacancy reuse, immediate direct Move, target departure, ranged-pursuit exclusion, final held-body clearance, and Tank steering around a held ally. No native assets or raw observations are included.
